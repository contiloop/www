# 0013 Elaboration is a relation kind

Context: the relation set had five kinds (cause, contrast, condition, consequence, question/answer). Two neighbouring claims the author wrote with no connective, where the second only tells more about the first, had no kind to take; the fixture record carried three such joins under kinds the author had not confirmed, and a draft rendered one of them with a condition the author never drew. The author asked for the supplement to be a relation of its own rather than a reason to fold the claim into a neighbouring step.

Decision: a sixth kind, `elaboration`: the next claim details the one before it (a definition, an example, the way something works) and the argument takes no step. It is recorded in parts 1, 2 and 3 like the other kinds, rendered with no causal or contrast connective or with one that announces detail, and the reading check does not count such a pair as a list. The declined mark stays for a join the author says has no relation at all.

Alternatives: folding a supplementary claim into the step it details (rejected by the author: a supplement is content of its own, not a step to erase); keeping five kinds and using the declined mark for supplements (loses the difference between "no relation" and "explains the previous claim").

Consequences: an author's statement with no connective can be recorded as what it is; the fixture's three unconfirmed joins stay as recorded until the author confirms or renames them, `elaboration` now among the choices.
