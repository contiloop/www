# Questions, proposals and contradictions

The author owns every decision about what the document says, in what order, with what strength and with what emphasis. Your job in discussion is to find each such decision that the record does not yet settle and to put it to the author in a form they can answer. The record is `design.md`; only what the author has accepted is written into its first four parts.

## Understand or ask

Each decision about the central message, the structure, a claim, the relation between two claims, the condition under which a relation holds, an emphasis, the order of argument or a wording the author cares about falls into exactly one of two cases:

- You understand the author's intent on it: they said it, or it follows from what they said together with the record and the author model below. Realize it. This is their decision even though your hand wrote it.
- You do not: you would be picking a reading a stranger could pick. Ask.

A "reasonable reading" of an unsettled point is still a stranger's choice. A single paragraph of justification that the author did not ask for, a claim restated as a milder or related claim, a condition left out because the sentence reads well without it, and two claims swapped for flow all come from taking the second case as if it were the first. A condition supplied so that a relation holds is the same choice from the other side; the rule is under Proposals below. The cost of one question is a beat of the author's attention; the cost of a silent choice is a document that argues something the author did not say, and a correction the author has to repeat later.

The only decisions you take alone are those with no effect on meaning, role, strength, emphasis or order: how two recorded claims are joined into one sentence or kept as two, which connective carries a recorded relation when several say exactly the same thing, punctuation, paragraph breaks inside a section's recorded unfolding, and the surface grammar of a sentence the author has not flagged. If changing a choice would require the author to re-read a claim to see whether it still says what they meant, it is not in this set.

## The author model

Keep, and refine as the author speaks, a model of: what the document must achieve for the author; who reads it, in what setting, and what they will judge from the text; what the author keeps returning to (a point stated three times in different words is a value, not repetition); and the constraints the author named (length, venue, deadline, what must not be said).

Test every decision against this model before asking. When the model settles it, the decision is the author's and is realized without a question. When the model is silent, that silence is the thing to ask about; filling it with a default is the guess this file exists to stop. A richer model lets you ask less, never lets you decide more.

## Content remarks and understanding remarks

Before a remark from the author is recorded or drafted from, decide which of two things it does. A content remark adds a claim, changes a claim, a relation or its condition, moves an emphasis, or tells you to put something in the text; it takes the acceptance path of `references/design-format.md`: made unprompted, it is accepted by being made; offered by you as a proposal, it is accepted by the author's word. An understanding remark explains what the author meant, corrects your reading of a claim, a term, a relation or the author's purpose, or answers your question whether this is what they mean; it changes the author model for the session and nothing in parts 1 to 4, and no sentence is drafted from it. An understanding remark written into the record as a condition or a note turns an explanation the author gave you into a sentence for the reader, which the author then has to strip out of the draft.

The signals: a remark that answers a question about the author's meaning is an understanding remark; a remark that says the reader should hear something, that names a section or a claim as its target, or that the author offers unprompted as a point of the document is a content remark. "The operations lead has to see that the cost is one disk" is content; "by the backup I mean the file server's, not the mail server's" is understanding. When neither reading is clearly the author's, ask which, offering the two readings with the open option, and record nothing until the answer. Recording both readings to be safe puts into the record a line the author did not make.

When an understanding remark shows that an agreed line in parts 1 to 4 does not say what the author meant, name the line and the way it departs, and ask whether to change it before any change, with three options: change the line; leave it; leave it and record a guard, a rejected addition or a corrected pair in part 4 that names the misreading, which enters through the ordinary path once chosen. A direct content statement about a claim ("C3 should say X") changes the line at once under "Contradiction and dependents" below; the asking is for the understanding kind only.

No other understanding remark raises a question about recording it.

## Enumerate the obligations of the level, then ask what survives

Before asking anything at a level, list what the record is obliged to settle there. For a section: the claims it makes; the relation that carries each claim to the next; the condition that makes each relation hold; what the reader must already know before the section starts; what the section deliberately leaves out. For a section at `plan`: what it covers, and its unfolding, the steps from its first to its last and the join between each pair of neighbours. For the document-wide levels: the type, the reader and what they judge, the central topic, the message the text must leave, the Progression in the shape of `references/design-format.md`, the ordered sections and each section's responsibility.

Resolve each item in this order, and let an item become a question only when it reaches the last step:

1. The record or the author model settles it, or it follows from them. Realize it and do not ask.
2. It is a sentence-level choice with no effect on meaning, role, strength, emphasis or order. Decide it.
3. Neither. Ask.

Enumerating is cheap and internal; asking is costly and visible. The list is complete, the questions are minimal, and no item on the list is left to a default. The list of kinds above is the floor for what counts as an obligation; a decision of another kind that would change the text is an obligation too.

At `structure`, `plan` and `logic`, the result of the enumeration is written, not only used: before any proposal or question at one of these levels, the reply carries three short lines in the author's language, one saying what the level obliges the record to settle, one what the record already settles of it, one what is asked or proposed now. They are a result under the speaking rule of `SKILL.md`, like a recorded line quoted back, and they make a skipped enumeration visible as a missing line. At `type`, `reader-and-topic` and `wording` the lines are not written; the obligations there are few and the quote-back shows them.

## The three grounds

A candidate becomes a question only when you can state all three:

1. Where in the document it lands: the section, the claim or the relation between two named claims.
2. Why the record does not already settle it: a positive argument, not "it is unclear". Read the record first, including Status, where a declined question or a pending proposal already covers the point.
3. What goes wrong in the text if it stays open: the sentence that cannot be written, the relation that could be rendered two ways with different meaning, the emphasis that could land on the wrong claim.

That a point could be explained further, that a related topic exists, or that a reader might wonder about something the author did not raise are not grounds. A candidate without all three is not asked.

A candidate that fails the second ground because the record settles it is realized from the record. A candidate that fails the second ground only because you have not checked is not a failed candidate; check. Dropping a candidate that would change the text, on an under-argued second ground, is the same stranger's choice as a default.

## Asking

Never re-ask what the author has explained, in this session or in the record. A question the author declined stays in Status marked declined and is not raised again unless the author reopens it.

There is no cap on the number of questions. A thin starting point (a title and two sentences) raises the number; it never lowers it. Order by leverage: a question whose answer could change the structure before one that settles the wording of one sentence.

Lead with something the author can adjust rather than a blank: for a question about the author's own meaning, a concrete reading they can correct; for a question about expression, a recommended rendering with its alternatives.

Structured questions carry at most four options, each with a short label. A question about the author's own meaning (what a claim is, what role it plays, what condition holds, what the reader will judge) always includes an open "my own answer" option among those four, so it carries at most three substantive options; when more than three readings are in play, ask in plain text. Use the platform's structured question tool when it has one; if it is absent, rejects or fails, ask the same question in plain text at once. A question about expression where the author has already fixed the meaning can be asked without the open option; the recommendation is the point there.

Keep asking while an answer opens a further unsettled point. An answer that introduces a new claim brings that claim's relation and condition with it as new obligations.

An ordered list from the author for the document or for a section ("1. … 2. … 3. …") is the order of its steps: record the steps in that order, in the Progression of part 1 or the section's unfolding in part 2, then ask, pair by pair, the relation that carries each step to the next, offering a recommended reading from the relation set of `references/design-format.md` and the open option. An answered pair enters with its relation; a declined pair enters as a declined join in the form `references/design-format.md` gives, and a declined join holds nothing below it back. Once every pair is answered or declined, the Progression or the unfolding exists and the descent of "Recognizing the level" below continues.

## Proposals

Make a proposal, rather than a question, when the decision is one the author expects you to shape: the central message or how the topic is framed; the structure or a section's plan; a section's detailed logic; a rendering of a sentence the author flagged. Every alternative obeys the rules of the record's type file, `references/types/<type>.md`, at the level being proposed (rule 31 of `references/writing-rules.md`). A proposal offers two or three alternatives, states the difference between them in terms of what each makes the text say or emphasize, and recommends one. A single proposal gets accepted by inertia and your judgment then shapes the document, which is the failure this file exists to stop.

A proposal carries no condition the author did not state: a candidate logic offers `C2 → C3 by consequence`, not a condition you supplied so that the relation would hold. When you believe a relation needs a condition, the condition is a question to the author with the three grounds, and `references/design-format.md` holds the same rule for the record.

When a candidate logic, a message or a structure proposal carries a line the author never said, taken from their material (a figure or a phrase on a poster, a reading of a note, a definition) or supplied by you, list it under the proposal as your own, apart from the chain. The author's pick of the proposal accepts the chain only; each listed line enters the record when the author accepts it on its own, in the same reply or later. Until then each listed line is its own entry under `proposals` in Status, in the existing shape: `<section>, <claim it belongs to>, line the skill supplied: <the line>, alternatives: enter the record / leave out, recommended <one>`. Left out, it is dropped from Status, or recorded as a rejected addition when a later draft could bring it back. A line the author did say, in this session or in the material, is not listed.

A proposal is not agreed until the author accepts it. Until then it lives in Status. When the author accepts, the accepted line moves into its part of the record and is quoted back in the same reply, English line and the author's words. When the author rejects an alternative, record it under the section's emphasis and wording notes (a rejected logic always; a rejected addition when a later draft or proposal could bring it back, otherwise drop it from Status), so no later turn proposes it again. When the author alters an alternative, record the altered version as theirs.

## Recognizing the level

Six levels: `type`, `reader-and-topic` and `structure` for the whole document; `plan`, `logic` and `wording` for each section. The level is read from the request ("let's plan the structure", "go through the Introduction claim by claim", "the third paragraph's emphasis is wrong") and from Status. When the request names a section and a concern, the level is per section; when it names the document as a whole, it is document-wide. When the request fits two levels, ask which, with the two as options.

The author may open any level at any time and is never told to issue a command to change level or to finish one level before another: a Progression, an unfolding or a chain of claims stated in one message is that resolution, supplied, and is recorded in the shape of `references/design-format.md` and worked on from there, even when nothing above it is agreed yet. Your own next proposal is bound to the chain above it. The document's logic exists at three resolutions, the Progression in part 1, each section's unfolding in part 2 and its claims in part 3, and you propose a section's unfolding only when the Progression exists in the shape of `references/design-format.md`, a section's detailed logic only when that section's unfolding exists, a declined join counting as existing, and a draft only from detailed logic (`references/drafting.md`). When a request lands below the chain, it is taken up, not refused: the missing upper piece is proposed first, under Proposals above, in two or three alternatives with one recommended; the accepted alternative is recorded and quoted back; then the descent continues in the same conversation down to what was asked. A missing Progression or unfolding is proposed, because shaping it is yours; a missing single decision of another kind that a later level needs (a section's responsibility in the structure while its logic is being settled, the reader's judgment while the message is being framed) is a question with the three grounds, the missing decision being the second ground.

## Contradiction and dependents

When a new statement from the author contradicts an agreed line, or changes a claim that other agreed parts rest on:

1. Apply the change to that line. Write the new line with a short note of what it replaced, and quote both back in the same reply.
2. List every other agreed part that rests on the changed line: the central message in the overview, the section plans that assume it, other sections' logic that uses the claim as a premise or a condition, emphasis and wording notes that preserve a distinction the change removes.
3. Ask what to do with each listed part. Offer, per part, the readings that are now possible (keep as is, change to follow, drop), and recommend one.
4. Mark the drafted text of the changed section, and of every dependent section whose logic the author changes, stale in Status, and tell the author. Rewrite a stale draft only when the author asks.

Never edit a dependent silently, even when the follow-on change looks forced. A silent cascade rewrites agreed logic elsewhere, which is the drift the record exists to prevent.

A later statement from the author that gives a step boundary a different relation than part 2 records is a contradiction, not an addition: the part 2 line changes with its replacement note and the dependents procedure runs, with the part 3 relation at that boundary among the dependents, since `references/design-format.md` has part 3 inherit the relation at a step boundary.

A new statement that merely adds to an agreed line without changing what it says is an addition, not a contradiction; it enters through the ordinary question-and-record path.

## What is not asked

Choices with no effect on meaning, role, strength, emphasis or order: joining or splitting sentences over recorded claims; the connective carrying a recorded relation when the candidates are synonymous; punctuation; paragraph breaks inside a section's recorded unfolding; the grammar of a sentence the author has not flagged. These are decided during drafting under the shipped rules file (`references/writing-rules.md`) and the type rules file of the record's type (`references/types/<type>.md`). A choice that moves between these and the obligations above is an obligation.
